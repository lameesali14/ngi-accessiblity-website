My Assessments

I assessed the National Art Gallery over the course of three days.

On the first day, I visited with my niece. I didn’t use the wheelchair yet — my intention was to get a general idea of how the National Art Gallery is laid out. I wanted to understand the space, observe where things were, and familiarize myself with the environment before returning to use the wheelchair.

I spoke to the information desk to confirm whether I would be allowed to film my experience. They informed me it would be fine as long as I didn’t film staff or other visitors. However, when I followed up in person, they clarified that filming was only permitted in the courtyard — not inside the gallery. I also tried contacting the filming department, but they didn’t respond, so I followed the guidance given by the visitor experience team. As a result, I only filmed in the courtyard and inside the elevator.

That day, I explored the gallery with my niece, located the elevators, and studied how to move through the space. I had access to maps online ahead of time, and physical maps were available at the front and information desks.

On the second day, I returned alone — intentionally — to experience what it would be like navigating the gallery independently in a wheelchair. I requested a wheelchair from the front desk, and they kindly provided it. I was instructed to return it when I was done, which I did.

I visited all the galleries I could. Most of the spaces were open and accessible. The security guards were kind and often offered help. One of my biggest challenges was getting through doors, as most of them were manual, not automatic. Since I was on my own, opening doors while in the wheelchair was physically difficult. Thankfully, security staff noticed when I was struggling and often helped without being asked.

One of the elevators was out of service that day, which prevented me from accessing part of the Grand Gallery. Staff were helpful and showed me the inaccessible area on the map ahead of time, so I was prepared. I couldn’t capture any footage that day because I arrived close to closing time. When I tried to film in the courtyard at the end, a security guard informed me the gallery was closing, so I stopped and returned the wheelchair. I went home exhausted, and my arms were sore from using a manual wheelchair all day — it was not electric, which made the experience physically demanding.

A week later, on the third day, I returned again. I asked for the wheelchair, which was kindly provided, and revisited the parts of the gallery I had missed before. I managed to capture a bit of footage in the courtyard using my phone. Filming myself while using the wheelchair felt a bit awkward, but I was able to document enough to showcase part of the experience. After filming, I returned the wheelchair and headed home.
