Features of NGI

Allowing Visitors with Disabilities to Get Around

Free to enter: the permanent collection and many temporary shows are open to everyone and don't need to be reserved. Carers can get free tickets to exhibitions. Two of our temporary exhibitions require paid tickets each year, but carers can enter for free with valid ID when accompanying the person they care for. Carers should book their free exhibition tickets ahead of time. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Parking

There are four disabled parking spots right outside the Gallery's Merrion Square entrance. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Entry Points

The Gallery has two entry points: one on Merrion Square West and the other on Clare Street. You can enter through either (or both) while visiting. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Access for Wheelchairs

All galleries and facilities are wheelchair and pram accessible. A limited number of wheelchairs are available to borrow. Email visitorexperienceteam@ngi.ie in advance or ask at the desk. Service and guide dogs are welcome in the Gallery. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Maps of Accessibility

Accessibility maps are available at information desks and online. You can find all of our maps here.

The Gallery’s Social Guide helps visitors plan their visit, showing what to expect and who you might meet. (PDF). (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Hearing Aids During Your Visit

Microphones have been installed at the information and membership desks to ensure safe, clear communication with staff. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Induction Loops for Hearing

Hearing aid loops are available at the front desks, in the lecture hall, and in the Gallery Shop.

Toilets

Accessible toilets are located in the Millennium Wing (Levels -1 and 1) and the Beit Wing (Level -1). A Changing Places bathroom is located in the Beit Wing (Level 1). Ask for the key at information desks or call 087 117 0880. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Large Print Booklets

Booklets with large-print artwork descriptions are available in the permanent collection galleries, in both English and Irish. Ask a staff member if you need help locating one. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Tours and Useful Links

The Gallery's Education team offers workshops, talks, and tours for all visitor needs. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Tours for Visitors with Hearing Difficulties

ISL tours are listed in the Gallery’s 'What’s On' calendar. Free guided tours for special needs groups are available. To book, email tours@ngi.ie at least two weeks in advance. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Home Resources for People with Dementia

Online tools are available to help people with dementia and their carers enjoy art from home. For info or to schedule a tour, email cmacconiomaire@ngi.ie. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Approval for Autism-Friendly Space

The National Gallery of Ireland has been accredited by AsIAm as an autism-friendly space — the first national cultural institution in Ireland to receive this recognition. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Access Officers Síle Boylan and Brina Casey are available for accessibility questions. You can reach them at accessibility@ngi.ie. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)
