About NGI

The Whole Organization

The National Gallery of Ireland Act of 1854 made the Gallery possible. There have been a number of Acts since then that have changed the law and clarified the institution's duties. These include the National Gallery of Ireland Acts (1854–1963) and the National Cultural Institutions Acts (1997–2023).

A Board of Governors and Guardians (the "Board") is in charge of running the Gallery according to its rules. The statutes created the role of Director, appointed by the Board, who is responsible for managing the institution.

The Gallery is a separate national cultural institution operating under the Department of Tourism, Culture, Arts, Gaeltacht, Sport, and Media. It consists of four main divisions: Corporate Services, Collections and Research, Audience Development and Stakeholder Engagement, and Collections and Research. (National Gallery of Ireland, n.d.)

Building an Audience and Involving Stakeholders

The Audience Development and Stakeholder Engagement division focuses on expanding public awareness of the Gallery through marketing, advertising, media engagement, and business planning. It also coordinates charitable work, fundraising, and capital campaigns.

This division includes Friends, Communications, Marketing and Digital Engagement (CMDE), Publications and Images, and Development. The goal is to engage national and international audiences using modern communication methods.

The Commercial department includes the Gallery Shop, Events team, Gallery Assistants, and partnerships with the Gallery Café. Their aim is to diversify income, support public programs, and create a stable funding model.

The CMDE team manages the Gallery's online presence through its website, social media, newsletters, and other channels. They promote events, programs, and exhibitions year-round.

The Development team raises funds through partnerships with businesses, individuals, foundations, and grant organisations. The Friends group supports Gallery work and acquisitions.

The Publications and Images team manages all printed and digital publications, image licensing, and intellectual property. Their Capture Digital Asset Management system hosts over 1,200 downloadable high-resolution images via the Creative Commons portal. (www.nationalgallery.ie, n.d.)

Collections and Research

This group includes the Library & Archives, Exhibitions & Collection Services, Education, and Conservation. The Gallery holds over 16,000 artworks from around 1300 to the present, and has a major art library and archive collection.

It includes works by Vermeer, Rembrandt, Goya, Caravaggio, Gainsborough, Monet, and Picasso, as well as the largest collection of Irish paintings and the National Portrait Collection.

The Collections and Research Division is responsible for preserving, growing, researching, and showcasing the collection. Their work is internationally recognized and involves high-profile partnerships.

Research plays a key role in conservation and interpretation. Study rooms, archives, and the art library are available to staff and the public. They share findings through publications, lectures, workshops, social media, and exhibitions. (www.nationalgallery.ie, n.d.)

Corporate Services

Corporate Services is responsible for governance, resource management, internal communication, and strategic planning. Key departments include:

  • Finance: Manages all financial operations, reporting, and budgeting.
  • HR: Oversees recruitment, training, wellbeing, and workplace policies.
  • IT: Maintains information systems, security, and digital infrastructure.
  • Legal & Compliance: Manages legal advice, codes of conduct, data protection, FOI, and risk management.
  • Procurement: Ensures compliance with procurement law and assists with tendering.
  • Safety & Security: Leads health and safety efforts and protects the collection with a supervisory and front-line security team.
