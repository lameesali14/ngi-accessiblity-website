Benefits and importance of Universal Design

An Approach for Universal Design

On many levels, the case for making our society easier for everyone to access and use is powerful. Universal Design is a way of building environments that everyone can access, understand, and fully use possible. It is an approach that is progressive and always changing. It makes good business sense to use Universal Design, and there are also strong legal and social reasons to do so. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Advantages for the Person

Universal Design supports a human-centred approach to design that is not only easy to use and convenient, but also protects the rights, privacy, and dignity of users. There are different levels of difficulty that people can have when using a product, service, or environment, such as A person who doesn't have any major issues but is interested in a well-designed product, service, or environment that is easy to access and use. Someone who does not have much trouble with any of the features. A person who has trouble with certain traits Anyone who has trouble with most of these traits Someone who cannot use the service or product at all. The level of personal benefit will depend on this. Because of this, everyone in the above groups will benefit if a product, service, or environment is well designed with usability and accessibility in mind. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Why universal design is good for society in a world that is changing

The world's population is changing a lot in terms of how old its people are. People are living longer thanks to advances in medicine and changes in how people live their lives. A look at some of the most important demographic changes that will happen in the next 30 years is shown below. The National Census of 2022 found that there were 25% more people over 80 years old than there were in the 2016 census. In the 2022 national census, 22% of people were disabled, up from 15% in the 2016 census. There will be twice as many people aged 65 and up in Ireland by 2051, or about 1.6 million. It is thought that children born today will live to be 100 years old. Within the next few decades, there will be a lot fewer people in Europe and Ireland who are willing and able to help and care for older people. The life expectancy of people with severe or multiple impairments is also going up, along with the number of people living with physical, sensory, mental health, intellectual, or neurodiverse conditions. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Living on Your Own

Universal design makes designs that work for everyone and encourages accessibility and usability so that people of all abilities can live on their own. How easily and quickly someone can get to and use goods, services, and places can affect their ability to stay as independent as possible. Universal design and other things that help people live on their own are very important in dealing with this worldwide problem. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

How Ability Works on a Scale

Universal Design is based on the idea that people's abilities are normal and not unique. Each person is unique, and no two are the same in terms of their skills. Different people are very different, and this difference can be caused by both inside and outside factors. Different people have different abilities depending on the activity they are doing or the setting in which they are doing it. Everyone has times in their lives when they cannot do as much as they used to. Like, "noisy environments can impair a person’s hearing; dimly lit rooms can impair a person’s vision; and having the flu can reduce a person’s stamina" Because of this, a Universal Design approach means taking into account that everyone has different abilities and designing a product, service, or environment that can be used by everyone, no matter their age, size, ability, or disability. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Taking part in society

In this age of technology, the skills people need to be a part of society are getting harder to learn. With each new technology that comes out, the chance that people who cannot or will not adopt it will be shut out of a wide range of government, social, cultural, and financial services or amenities grows. It is getting harder to tell the difference between products, ICT, and building design because technology is being used in more and more places and in more and more things. To make society more accessible for people of all abilities, ages, and sizes, buildings and systems must be designed with the user in mind from the start, not just during user trials. A space that is designed to be usable by everyone promotes equality and makes everyone's life easier and safer. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Advantages for Business in a Market That Is Changing

Business can get a lot of benefits from using universal design, such as reaching more customers and making them happier. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

More people in the market

The goal of universal design is to make designs that as many people as possible can access, use, and like. One effect of this is that the market will reach more people. Not only could more potential customers get a product, service, or environment, but they could also get a wider range of potential customers. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Better customer retention and satisfaction

A happy customer will tell others about the product, service, or environment, which could lead to more people knowing about it and bringing in new customers. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Market Crossover That Works

Products made for a certain group of people can sometimes get interest and demand from markets that were not expected. A popular example of how a unique product design (made with older people with arthritis in mind) can lead to high demand. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Good Image in Public

Businesses that use universal design to make the world a better place is likely to be known for having a high level of corporate social responsibility.(Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Higher expectations for consumers

The voice of the people has become louder and more important over the last few decades. IT (information and communication technologies) and social media have made it easier for people to voice their complaints. They are more likely to do so now. Many businesses have changed how they design products and how they treat customers to better serve a wider range of people. This has been prompted by both direct pressure from consumer groups and direct interaction with their customers. Universal Design lets businesses make products, services, and spaces that are more in line with what customers want and need. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Ready to Use Right Away

People decide what they think about a product after they use it or even just see it for the first time. A new study suggests that people use the internet in less than 50 milliseconds (one twentieth of a second) to decide if a website looks good. People are not likely to buy something if they have a bad first impression of it. People will be more likely to buy something, though, if it is simple, clear, easy to get to, and easy to use. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)

Follow through with laws and standards

Guidelines and standards give people practical advice on how to follow the law. In standards and guidelines, the minimum requirements and specifics often include suggestions for making things easier to use and accessible. Universal Design is a way of designing a product, service, or environment that not only helps people follow the rules, but it can also make things easier for everyone to use and access, above and beyond what the law requires. (Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, n.d.)
